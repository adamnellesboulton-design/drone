NES DRONE — CONTROLS REFERENCE
================================


SA (note / Hz)
The root pitch in Hz — cycles per second. 440 Hz is A4, the standard tuning
reference. Doubling the Hz goes up an octave; halving goes down one. Abbas sets
this to match his tabla tuning.

KATTAI
A South Indian classical numbering system for pitch. C = 1, C# = 1.5, D = 2,
and so on up to B = 6.5. Just a reference readout — same pitch, different
notation.


MASTER VOL
Linear gain from 0 to 1.0 applied after the NES mixer. 0.8 default leaves
headroom. No units beyond that — it's a straight multiplier on the output
sample.


P1 / P2 VOL
Integer values 0–15. This is a 4-bit hardware register — only 16 possible
loudness levels exist, nothing between them. The NES doesn't have fine-grained
volume; it has 16 steps. The nonlinear mixer means the difference between
adjacent steps isn't perceptually equal — the jump from 1→2 is more audible
than 14→15.

P1 / P2 DUTY
Three fixed options: 12.5%, 25%, 50%. These describe what fraction of each wave
cycle the signal spends high versus low. A 50% duty cycle is a true square wave
— equal time high and low. 25% spends a quarter of the cycle high. 12.5% is an
eighth. The timbre difference is real: 25% has a hollow, woody quality; 12.5%
is thin and reedy; 50% is fuller but loses some of the characteristic NES edge.

SHIMMER (P2)
Cents. One cent is one hundredth of a semitone — there are 100 cents between
any two adjacent notes on a piano, and 1200 cents in an octave. 7¢ is a very
small interval, not perceptible as a pitch difference on its own, but when two
tones are that close together they interfere and produce a beating pattern — a
slow amplitude fluctuation at the rate of their frequency difference. At A3
(220 Hz), 7¢ offset produces a beat of roughly 0.9 Hz, or about one pulse per
second. That slow pulse is the jiva string effect.


DRIFT DEPTH
Cents — peak deviation of the pitch LFO from center. At 8¢ (default) the pitch
wanders up to 8 cents sharp and 8 cents flat of the target frequency. Since the
NES period register is an integer, the actual pitch is already slightly off from
true equal temperament — the drift sits on top of that quantization and masks it
perceptually. 12¢ (tight max) is just over a tenth of a semitone — audible as
movement, not as pitch wrongness.

DRIFT SPEED
Hz — cycles per second of the LFO. 0.13 Hz means one complete drift cycle takes
about 7.7 seconds. The three-partial LFO structure means the actual contour
isn't a clean sine — it's a sum of three sinusoids at ratios 1 : 0.55 : 0.37,
which produces an irregular, non-repeating shape. The tight range of 0.08–0.20
Hz keeps the cycle between 5 and 12 seconds, which is roughly the timescale of
a breath or a tanpura stroke.

SWELL
A fraction from 0 to 1 representing how far the volume can drop from its set
level. At 0.45 (default), the pulse volume can fall to 55% of its set value at
the bottom of the swell cycle. Because volume is 4-bit, the actual value is
rounded to the nearest integer — so at vol=13 with swell=0.45, the bottom of
the swell is round(13 × 0.55) = 7, and the volume steps between 7 and 13 over
the cycle. The floor never reaches 0 because the swell multiplier doesn't reach
zero.

SWELL SPD
Hz, same as drift speed. 0.18 Hz default is about a 5.5-second cycle —
slightly faster than drift so the two don't lock in phase.


VIBRATO DEPTH
Cents, peak deviation, applied globally across all three channels as a clean
sine LFO. Unlike drift (aperiodic, per-channel, slow) this is a regular
oscillation. 6¢ is the tight maximum — half the shimmer detune, barely
perceptible as pitch movement but enough to add life if the drone sounds too
static.

VIBRATO RATE
Hz. 4–7 Hz tight range. Human vocal vibrato typically sits at 5–6 Hz; sitar
vibrato is similar. Below 4 Hz the ear starts to track it as deliberate pitch
movement (andolan territory). Above 7 Hz it starts to sound mechanical or like
flutter rather than vibrato.


WIDE (toggle)
Extends all the cent and Hz ranges above their practice defaults. No new units
— just higher ceilings on the same parameters.
